## Full description (4000 char max)

```
Adventure Day is a gentle exploring game made for a five-year-old, by her dad.

There is nothing to win and nothing to lose. Nothing chases her, nothing runs
out, and nothing is ever timed. She can put it down mid-sentence and pick it up
a week later exactly where she left off.

WHAT SHE DOES

Sweep the meadow with a metal detector and dig up ammonites, old coins, shark
teeth and dinosaur bones. Take them home and arrange them on the shelves of her
own museum, exactly how she likes them.

Build a treehouse. Really build it — carry the wood home, choose what to make
next from the plan board, and watch her hammer it together piece by piece.
Three trees, more than sixty things to build, from a rope ladder to a spiral
staircase to a crow's nest with a spyglass.

Take photographs of the animals. Real photographs of real robins, frogs,
butterflies and owls appear in her album. Talk to them first and they will tell
her something true: a frog drinks through its skin, an owl has one ear higher
than the other.

Ride her bike between five places. A market where she barters fruit for timber
and paint. Dino Land, where four kinds of dinosaur amble about and there is a
cave with a grumpy T-rex in it who only needs somebody to pull the splinter out
of his foot. A pirate cove with ships in the bay and a treasure map in four
pieces.

Make a sandwich as tall as she likes and sit down on the blanket to eat it.
Everything in the picnic is vegan, and the ones that would normally come from an
animal wear a little green leaf badge, so she can see that nothing in her lunch
came from one.

Draw on the sketch pad. Grow a garden. Whistle for the dogs — Indy and Star
come running from wherever they are.

FIVE LITTLE GAMES, HIDDEN AROUND THE WORLD

Counting fruit on a market stall. Matching bones. Finding the pattern in a row
of flowers. Building a word to walk the plank. A memory game up in the museum.
Each one is worth doing for what it gives her, and none of them can be failed —
a wrong answer wobbles and asks again, and the game quietly gets easier if she
is finding it hard.

FOR PARENTS

- No adverts. No analytics. No tracking. No third-party SDKs.
- No internet permission at all — the app cannot go online.
- No in-app purchases and nothing to buy.
- Nothing is collected about your child, ever.
- Reading level is adjustable: pictures only, single words, or full sentences,
  so it grows with her. Words are never required to play.
- Nothing she collects can ever be destroyed by accident.

Made for Summer, who is nearly six.
```
